# Abstract

Sentinel lymph node assessment on hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) sections is central to breast cancer staging but remains time-consuming and variable between observers. Patch-level machine learning offers scalable decision support, yet models trained in one centre or stain regime often degrade when scanners, reagents, or tissue appearance change—problems usually grouped under domain shift.

This thesis builds a reproducible patch-level framework on PatchCamelyon (PCam) and CAMELYON17 (WILDS) under the public train, validation, and test splits. A fixed pipeline combines within-PCam deduplication, cross-dataset hash screening, quality filtering, and single-reference Macenko stain normalization (chosen after a prespecified shallow-CNN stain benchmark). Hyperparameters, checkpoints, and post-hoc temperature scaling use only the source validation split; all held-out test material is reserved for reporting. Two architectures are compared under the same tensors and transfer layout: a compact convolutional neural network trained end-to-end, and Virchow2 with a frozen encoder and trainable linear head.

The study separates what preprocessing fixes from what representation capacity fixes. On raw catalogue patches, a CAMELYON17-trained shallow CNN reached only moderate in-domain ranking (ROC-AUC 0.724) and recall 0.16 at probability 0.5, showing that uncorrected inputs can hide usable capacity. After the study pipeline, the same architecture reached in-domain CAMELYON17 ROC-AUC 0.985 and recall 0.91 at 0.5, with clear gains on PCam as well. Virchow2 outperformed the CNN on preprocessed test splits in every transfer cell, with the hardest asymmetry when training on CAMELYON17 and testing on PCam: ranking remained strong (ROC-AUC 0.950), but recall at 0.5 fell to 0.630 with a large false-negative pool on the full test split. Post-hoc temperature scaling improved log loss and ECE\(_{15}\) in that stressed setting but did not restore recall at the fixed 0.5 operating point; in-domain PCam calibration (ECE\(_{15}\) 0.020) contrasted with external PCam after CAMELYON17 training (0.160). Bootstrap percentile intervals and paired permutation tests (with multiplicity control) supported planned comparisons. A structured checklist review on small, prespecified exports linked failure modes to tissue context and morphology; among reviewed high-entropy errors, borderline morphology was common—a sampled pattern, not a full-test census.

Together, the results argue that ranking metrics and threshold-based behaviour can diverge under transfer, that integrity-aware preprocessing and foundation-model features address different bottlenecks, and that reporting calibration and operational metrics—not ROC alone—matters for cross-benchmark patch classifiers. They do not constitute a general solution to domain shift or a claim of uniform generalization beyond these splits.

# Conclusion

The work delivered an auditable, end-to-end recipe for patch-level metastasis classification on two public H&E benchmarks, with explicit controls for duplicates, overlap, quality, stain normalization, and validation-bound model selection and calibration. Empirically, preprocessing was decisive for a shallow CNN on difficult inputs—most visibly on CAMELYON17—while Virchow2’s frozen representation improved discrimination and calibration across the same four transfer cells. Bidirectional testing remained asymmetric: transfer from PCam to external CAMELYON17 was strong for both model families, whereas CAMELYON17-trained models faced a harder external PCam direction, where high ROC-AUC coexisted with low recall at 0.5 and worse probability reliability despite temperature scaling. Qualitative review supported those quantitative themes on exported subsets but did not replace full-test metrics.

What the evidence cannot claim by design, how GPU time and wall-clock limits bounded optional extensions, and which directions follow next are set out in the dedicated Limitations and Future Work chapter so they are not duplicated here.

This thesis does not put forward a general solution to domain shift in histopathology. It contributes a prespecified, auditable pipeline and bidirectional evaluation on two public patch benchmarks, together with evidence that measured cross-domain performance improves in specific, documented comparisons: the study pipeline markedly changed what a shallow CNN could learn on difficult CAMELYON17 inputs, and Virchow2 improved over that CNN on the same preprocessed tensors across all four transfer cells. Those statements are intentionally narrow. They describe behaviour on fixed splits and label definitions, not guaranteed robustness to every new centre, scanner, or slide-level workflow. “Generalization” in the sense of uniformly safe external use was not demonstrated and should not be inferred from strong ROC–AUC alone when threshold recall and calibration still degrade on the hardest transfer direction.

In sum, the thesis supports a practical validation stance: layer data-integrity and preprocessing with strong encoders, and when benchmarks differ in stain, hospital mix, and prevalence, pair ranking summaries with threshold and calibration views and document how operating points were chosen—so that “good AUC” is not mistaken for safe behaviour under shift.
